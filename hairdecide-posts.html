"Bond Repair" — What It Actually Does (And Doesn't)

Thu Jan 8Single Post
Marketing vs. Molecule
"Bond repair"
— what does
it actually do?
STRUCTURAL (real)
Bis-Aminopropyl Diglycol Dimaleate reforms disulfide bonds. Permanent repair.
COSMETIC (marketing)
Hydrolysed protein coats the hair shaft. Temporary. Washes out.
Full Caption
Every hair brand has a "bond repair" product. Most of them are lying about what they repair. There are two types of bonds in hair: 1. HYDROGEN BONDS Broken by water. Temporarily reformed when hair dries. This is how blow-drying and heat styling work — you're just rearranging hydrogen bonds. Every wash disrupts them. Every dry restores them. No product "repairs" these because they repair themselves. 2. DISULFIDE BONDS Broken by: bleach, chemical colourants, perms, relaxers, keratin treatments, and repeated high heat. These breaks are permanent. Disulfide bonds are what give your hair its shape and strength. TRUE structural bond repair = reforming disulfide bonds. The molecule that does this: Bis-Aminopropyl Diglycol Dimaleate (the Olaplex patent). There are now a few legitimate alternatives on the market. Everything else called "bond repair": Hydrolysed protein (keratin, wheat, silk): coats the hair shaft, fills gaps temporarily, improves feel. Washes out. Cosmetic — not structural. Both have value. But knowing which you're buying is the difference between managing damage and actually repairing it. HairDecide's Claims Decoder identifies which bond chemistry is in your product. Link in bio.

Curly Hair Is Structurally Dry — Here's The Biology

Sat Jan 10Carousel · 4 slides
01/04 · Hair Type Science
Curly hair is
structurally dry.
Not because you're doing it wrong.
Because of physics.
The physics:
Sebum travels down the hair shaft.
A spiral shaft = sebum can't travel far.
Straight hair: fully coated.
Coily hair: mostly uncoated.
Result: moisture products, not oil-stripping ones.
Full Caption
Curly and coily hair isn't dry because you're not moisturising enough. It's dry because of the physics of the hair shaft. Here's why: Sebum is produced at the scalp's sebaceous glands and travels down the hair shaft to naturally condition it. On a straight hair shaft, sebum travels the full length easily — one reason straight hair can go oily quickly. On a curly or coily shaft, sebum has to travel around each twist and curl. By the time it reaches the mid-shaft and ends — it doesn't. The ends of coily hair type 4 may be completely sebum-free. This is structural, not behavioural. Curly hair is inherently drier. Your routine needs to work with this physics. What this means: → Avoid sulfate shampoos that strip the limited sebum that does reach the shaft → Prioritise deep conditioning (Behentrimonium Chloride, fatty acids, glycerin) → Seal with a butter or oil to prevent water loss → Apply products to wet hair — let them lock in the moisture → Heat (shower cap + warm towel) improves conditioner penetration by opening the cuticle HairDecide's profile builder matches your hair type to evidence-based ingredient recommendations. Dandruff ≠ Dry Scalp. You're Treating The Wrong Thing.

Tue Jan 13Reel · 30s
Myth Decoder
Dandruff
≠
dry scalp.
If you moisturise your scalp to treat dandruff, you're feeding the yeast.
The actual cause:
Malassezia yeast overproduction
→ oleic acid → scalp inflammation
→ rapid cell turnover → flakes
Full Caption (Reel)
If you're moisturising your scalp to treat dandruff, you're making it worse. Dandruff is not dry scalp. It's a fungal condition. The cause: Malassezia — a yeast that lives on the scalps of all humans. In most people, it's controlled. In some, it overproduces and breaks down sebum into oleic acid. Oleic acid triggers an inflammatory response in sensitive scalps. That inflammation causes rapid skin cell turnover. The excess dead cells = dandruff flakes. Adding oil or moisture does NOT help. Malassezia feeds on lipids. More sebum/oil = more Malassezia food. The correct treatment: → Zinc Pyrithione 1% (OTC): antifungal + antibacterial. Use 3× per week, leave 3–5 min, then rinse. Contact time is what kills the yeast. → Ketoconazole 2% (prescription in most EU countries): most effective antifungal. 2× per week for 4 weeks, then maintenance. → Selenium sulfide: effective but more irritating. The mistake: treating dandruff like dry skin. The fix: antifungal + patience. HairDecide identifies antifungal actives in your shampoo. Link in bio.

"Sulfate-Free" Is Fear-Based Marketing. Here's The Nuance.

Thu Jan 15Single Post
Marketing vs. Molecule
"Sulfate-free"
is fear-based
marketing.
SLS (sodium lauryl sulfate)Strong · For oily/fine
SLES (sodium laureth)Milder · Most hair types
Cocamidopropyl BetaineGentlest · Dry/colour/curly
Full Caption
"Sulfate-free" has become a marker of premium haircare. It shouldn't be. It's a spectrum — not a binary. Sulfates are surfactants — they clean by attracting both water and oil. Sodium Lauryl Sulfate (SLS) is the strongest. Sodium Laureth Sulfate (SLES) is milder. Both are effective. Both are well-studied. Neither is inherently harmful. The question is: what does your scalp and hair actually need? SLS — appropriate for: → Oily scalp / fine hair that gets weighed down fast → Product build-up removal (silicone build-up, dry shampoo residue) → Scalp with dandruff (strong cleansing helps) SLES — appropriate for: → Normal to moderately oily scalp → Most hair types, including coloured (milder on colour) Sulfate-free (Cocamidopropyl Betaine, Coco-Glucoside): → Dry, curly, coily, or bleached hair → Scalp sensitivity or eczema → If you're co-washing "Sulfate-free" marketing implies sulfates are universally bad. They aren't — they're just not right for every hair type. HairDecide's profile tells you which surfactant class is right for your scalp concern. Link in bio.
